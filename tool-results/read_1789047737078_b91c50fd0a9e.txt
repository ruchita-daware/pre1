   205→   205→CONTEXT
   206→   206→PreOne is a multi-tenant SaaS platform serving the preschool education market
   207→   207→across India. The platform must support hundreds of schools, thousands of
   208→   208→branches, and millions of students, parents, teachers, and administrators while
   209→   209→complying with stringent child-data protection regulations. Before v1.0,
   210→   210→architectural decisions were made ad-hoc by individual feature teams, leading
   211→   211→to fragmentation: three different caching strategies, two different
   212→   212→authentication mechanisms, and inconsistent API conventions across modules.
   213→   213→The Office of the Chief Architect was established in Q2 2025 to unify
   214→   214→architectural direction. The first deliverable was a set of canonical principles
   215→   215→that every team would align to. These principles are not technology choices —
   216→   216→they are value statements that bound the solution space for every downstream
   217→   217→ADR. Technology choices (Java 21, PostgreSQL 16, etc.) are captured in
   218→   218→ADR-020 and must comply with these principles. The principles were drafted
   219→   219→through a series of workshops with engineering, product, security, and
   220→   220→operations leadership over six weeks. Each principle was stress-tested against
   221→   221→real PreOne scenarios: a school onboarding 500 students in a single day, a
   222→   222→parent accessing their child's report card on mobile, a regulator auditing data
   223→   223→retention. The 10 principles below are the result of that stress-testing.
   224→   224→BUSINESS DRIVERS
   225→   225→The primary business driver is platform scalability: PreOne must scale from 50
   226→   226→schools (current) to 5,000 schools (5-year target) without architectural rework.
   227→   227→Fragmented architecture cannot scale because each new team reinvents
   228→   228→foundations, multiplying operational cost. Unified principles compress the
   229→   229→decision space so that new teams build on the same foundations as existing
   230→   230→teams. The secondary driver is compliance: preschool data is subject to
   231→   231→multiple regulations (DPDP Act 2023, COPPA-equivalent norms, state-level
   232→   232→child protection laws). Principles that bake privacy-by-design and audit-by-
   233→   233→default into every decision reduce the compliance burden per feature. The
   234→   234→tertiary driver is onboarding speed: new engineers can be productive in 2-3
   235→   235→PreOne ADR Series - Phase 2 / 10 - Vol 1: Architecture Foundation - 6
   236→   236→

   238→   238→weeks when principles are explicit, versus 8-12 weeks when they must reverse-
   239→   239→engineer conventions from code.
   240→   240→PROBLEM STATEMENT
   241→   241→Architectural decisions across PreOne feature teams are inconsistent, leading
   242→   242→to operational fragmentation, compliance risk, and slow onboarding. We need a
   243→   243→small set of canonical principles that every team aligns to, enforced through
   244→   244→code review and ADR governance.
   245→   245→CONSTRAINTS
   246→   246→● Principles must be technology-agnostic — they outlive any specific
   247→   247→stack choice
   248→   248→● Principles must be testable — each must have a concrete verification
   249→   249→mechanism
   250→   250→● Principles must be memorable — engineers should be able to recite
   251→   251→them without lookup
   252→   252→● Principles must be ordered — when two principles conflict, the earlier
   253→   253→one takes precedence
   254→   254→● Principles must be applicable to both greenfield and brownfield work

   315→   315→ADOPTED
   316→   316→We adopt the following 10 canonical architecture principles, in precedence
   317→   317→order. Every ADR in the PreOne corpus must explicitly cite which
   318→   318→principle(s) it serves, and the ARB must verify alignment during review. (1)
   319→   319→Tenant isolation is non-negotiable. (2) Data integrity over performance. (3)
   320→   320→Security by default, not by retrofit. (4) Observability is a feature, not an
   321→   321→afterthought. (5) Simplicity over cleverness. (6) Build for evolution, not for
   322→   322→prediction. (7) Contracts before code. (8) Fail loud, fail fast, fail safe. (9)
   323→   323→Automate everything repeatable. (10) Document every decision that
   324→   324→survives a week.
   325→   325→DETAILED RATIONALE
   326→   326→The 10 principles were chosen because they collectively cover the full surface
   327→   327→area of architectural concern at PreOne without overlap. Tenant isolation (P1)
   328→   328→is first because a multi-tenant SaaS that leaks data between tenants is not just a
   329→   329→bug — it is an existential business risk; everything else is secondary if isolation
   330→   330→fails. Data integrity (P2) precedes performance (covered implicitly in P5 and
   331→   331→P6) because a fast platform that corrupts data is worse than a slow platform
   332→   332→that preserves it; performance can be optimised, but trust lost to corruption
   333→   333→cannot be regained. Security (P3) and observability (P4) are placed before
   334→   334→simplicity (P5) because PreOne handles child data — a single breach is a
   335→   335→regulatory and reputational event that could end the company. Simplicity is
   336→   336→valued but never at the expense of security or observability. Build for evolution
   337→   337→(P6) reflects the reality that PreOne's product direction will shift; architectures
   338→   338→that lock in current assumptions (e.g., hard-coding academic year as a single
   339→   339→global value) create expensive rework when the assumption breaks. Contracts
   340→   340→before code (P7) ensures that API and data contracts are designed before
   341→   341→implementation, preventing the common failure mode where the contract is
   342→   342→reverse-engineered from the code (and thus inherits all the code's accidents).
   343→   343→Fail loud (P8) rejects silent failure modes — exceptions that are swallowed,
   344→   344→defaults that mask misconfiguration — because silent failures in a child-data
   345→   345→platform are how breaches happen. Automate everything repeatable (P9) is the
   346→   346→principle that justifies the CI/CD, IaC, and testing investments in Volumes 6 and
   347→   347→8. Document every decision (P10) is the meta-principle that justifies the ADR
   348→   348→corpus itself. The most likely counter-argument is that 10 principles are too
   349→   349→many to enforce rigorously. The counter-counter-argument is that enforcement
   350→   350→is not via human vigilance but via the ADR workflow: every Proposed ADR must
   351→   351→list which principles it serves (or explicitly state it does not serve any, which is
   352→   352→itself a flag for ARB review). This makes enforcement mechanical, not
   353→   353→discretionary.

   400→   400→No direct database impact. The principles are abstract and do not prescribe
   401→   401→schema, storage, or indexing choices. However, P1 (Tenant Isolation) and P2
   402→   402→PreOne ADR Series - Phase 2 / 10 - Vol 1: Architecture Foundation - 10
   403→   403→

   405→   405→(Data Integrity) directly constrain every database ADR in Volume 3: ADR-043
   406→   406→(Multi-Tenant), ADR-044 (School Isolation), ADR-047 (Soft Delete), and
   407→   407→ADR-048 (Audit Trail) are all implementations of these principles at the data
   408→   408→layer. The principle citation in each Volume 3 ADR's Context section creates a
   409→   409→traceable link from concrete schema choices back to foundational values.
   410→   410→API IMPACT
   411→   411→No direct API impact. However, P7 (Contracts Before Code) is the foundational
   412→   412→principle for Volume 5 (API & Integration). Every API ADR — ADR-091 (REST),
   413→   413→ADR-092 (Versioning), ADR-093 (Error Codes), ADR-106 (Idempotency) — cites
   414→   414→P7 in its rationale. The principle enforces the workflow: OpenAPI specs are
   415→   415→reviewed and merged before implementation PRs are accepted; a PR that
   416→   416→introduces an endpoint not in the spec is blocked by CI.
   417→   417→UI IMPACT
   418→   418→No direct UI impact. However, P4 (Observability) and P8 (Fail Loud) influence
   419→   419→UI behaviour: errors are surfaced to users with clear, actionable messages (not
   420→   420→silent failures); loading states are explicit (not blank screens); offline states are
   421→   421→communicated (ADR-142). P5 (Simplicity) governs UI design system choices in
   422→   422→Volume 7 — component count is kept minimal, composition is preferred over
   423→   423→configuration.
   424→   424→SECURITY IMPACT
   425→   425→P3 (Security by Default) is the foundational security principle. Every Volume 4
   426→   426→ADR must cite P3. The principle means: authentication is required by default
   427→   427→(opt-out requires ARB approval); encryption is enabled by default; audit logging
   428→   428→is on by default; rate limiting is on by default. Opting out of any default security
   429→   429→control requires an ADR that justifies the exception and is approved by both the
   430→   430→Domain Architect and the Security Officer. This inverts the traditional model
   431→   431→where security is added later — at PreOne, security is the starting point and

   433→   433→PERFORMANCE IMPACT
   434→   434→P2 (Data Integrity over Performance) explicitly accepts performance trade-offs
   435→   435→in favour of correctness. For example, we choose serialisable transaction
   436→   436→isolation (not read-committed) for financial data, accepting the latency cost. P5
   437→   437→(Simplicity) reduces performance overhead by avoiding over-engineering — no
   438→   438→premature optimisation, no speculative caching. Performance optimisation is
   439→   439→permitted only when (a) a measured bottleneck is documented, (b) the
   440→   440→optimisation does not violate P1-P4, and (c) the optimisation is captured in an

   515→   515→REJECTED ALTERNATIVES
   516→   516→The 25-principle option was rejected because cognitive load tests showed
   517→   517→engineers could recite at most 7-10 principles from memory; beyond that, they
   518→   518→look them up, which means the principles function as documentation rather
   519→   519→than as internalised values. The 5-principle option was rejected because the 5
   520→   520→candidates (isolation, integrity, security, observability, simplicity) were too
   521→   521→abstract to resolve common disputes — for example, 'contracts before code' and
   522→   522→'fail loud' are both expressions of simplicity but guide different concrete
   523→   523→decisions. The no-principles option was rejected because the pre-v1.0 state of
   524→   524→the platform demonstrated that ad-hoc decisions produce fragmentation; the
   525→   525→cost of unifying later (which we paid in v1.0) exceeds the cost of aligning
   526→   526→upfront.

   538→   538→TESTING STRATEGY
   539→   539→Principles are tested through the ADR workflow: every Proposed ADR must cite
   540→   540→at least one principle in its Context section, and the ARB verifies alignment
   541→   541→during review. Additionally, CI pipeline checks enforce principles
   542→   542→mechanically: P3 (security defaults) via SAST rules that fail builds on missing

   545→   545→merge. P5 (simplicity) is tested through code review with a rubric. Principle
   546→   546→alignment is reported quarterly in the architecture scorecard.

   170→Architecture Foundation
   171→ADR-001 to ADR-020 - 20 decisions - 34 sections each
   172→Volume 1 defines the foundational architectural principles, structural
   173→rules, and technology stack that all subsequent volumes build upon. The
   174→20 ADRs in this volume are the most-referenced in the entire 160-ADR
   175→corpus. ADR-001 establishes 10 canonical principles that govern every
   176→subsequent decision. ADR-002 through ADR-013 define the structural
   177→patterns: modular monolith, clean architecture, DDD, bounded contexts,
   178→layering, dependency rules, SOLID, hexagonal architecture, package
   179→structure, coding standards, naming standards, and project structure.
   180→ADR-014 through ADR-016 define the operational foundation:
   181→configuration hierarchy, environment strategy, and feature flags.
   182→ADR-017 through ADR-020 define the delivery foundation: build pipeline,
   183→deployment model, versioning policy, and the technology stack freeze.
   184→Together, these 20 ADRs are the constitution of the PreOne platform.

   205→CONTEXT
   206→PreOne is a multi-tenant SaaS platform serving the preschool education market
   207→across India. The platform must support hundreds of schools, thousands of
   208→branches, and millions of students, parents, teachers, and administrators while
   209→complying with stringent child-data protection regulations. Before v1.0,
   210→architectural decisions were made ad-hoc by individual feature teams, leading
   211→to fragmentation: three different caching strategies, two different
   212→authentication mechanisms, and inconsistent API conventions across modules.
   213→The Office of the Chief Architect was established in Q2 2025 to unify
   214→architectural direction. The first deliverable was a set of canonical principles
   215→that every team would align to. These principles are not technology choices —
   216→they are value statements that bound the solution space for every downstream
   217→ADR. Technology choices (Java 21, PostgreSQL 16, etc.) are captured in
   218→ADR-020 and must comply with these principles. The principles were drafted
   219→through a series of workshops with engineering, product, security, and
   220→operations leadership over six weeks. Each principle was stress-tested against
   221→real PreOne scenarios: a school onboarding 500 students in a single day, a
   222→parent accessing their child's report card on mobile, a regulator auditing data
   223→retention. The 10 principles below are the result of that stress-testing.
   224→BUSINESS DRIVERS
   225→The primary business driver is platform scalability: PreOne must scale from 50
   226→schools (current) to 5,000 schools (5-year target) without architectural rework.
   227→Fragmented architecture cannot scale because each new team reinvents
   228→foundations, multiplying operational cost. Unified principles compress the
   229→decision space so that new teams build on the same foundations as existing
   230→teams. The secondary driver is compliance: preschool data is subject to
   231→multiple regulations (DPDP Act 2023, COPPA-equivalent norms, state-level
   232→child protection laws). Principles that bake privacy-by-design and audit-by-
   233→default into every decision reduce the compliance burden per feature. The
   234→tertiary driver is onboarding speed: new engineers can be productive in 2-3
   235→PreOne ADR Series - Phase 2 / 10 - Vol 1: Architecture Foundation - 6
   236→

   238→weeks when principles are explicit, versus 8-12 weeks when they must reverse-
   239→engineer conventions from code.
   240→PROBLEM STATEMENT
   241→Architectural decisions across PreOne feature teams are inconsistent, leading
   242→to operational fragmentation, compliance risk, and slow onboarding. We need a
   243→small set of canonical principles that every team aligns to, enforced through
   244→code review and ADR governance.
   245→CONSTRAINTS
   246→● Principles must be technology-agnostic — they outlive any specific
   247→stack choice
   248→● Principles must be testable — each must have a concrete verification
   249→mechanism
   250→● Principles must be memorable — engineers should be able to recite
   251→them without lookup
   252→● Principles must be ordered — when two principles conflict, the earlier
   253→one takes precedence
   254→● Principles must be applicable to both greenfield and brownfield work
   255→ASSUMPTIONS
   256→● PreOne will remain a multi-tenant SaaS platform for the foreseeable
   257→future (no pivot to on-premise)
   258→● The engineering organisation will grow from ~40 engineers (current)
   259→to ~150 engineers (3-year target)
   260→● The regulatory environment will tighten, not loosen, over the next 5
   261→years
   262→● Cloud infrastructure (AWS) will remain the deployment substrate
   263→● The platform will continue to serve the preschool education market (no
   264→expansion to K-12 in scope)

   315→ADOPTED
   316→We adopt the following 10 canonical architecture principles, in precedence
   317→order. Every ADR in the PreOne corpus must explicitly cite which
   318→principle(s) it serves, and the ARB must verify alignment during review. (1)
   319→Tenant isolation is non-negotiable. (2) Data integrity over performance. (3)
   320→Security by default, not by retrofit. (4) Observability is a feature, not an
   321→afterthought. (5) Simplicity over cleverness. (6) Build for evolution, not for
   322→prediction. (7) Contracts before code. (8) Fail loud, fail fast, fail safe. (9)
   323→Automate everything repeatable. (10) Document every decision that
   324→survives a week.
   325→DETAILED RATIONALE
   326→The 10 principles were chosen because they collectively cover the full surface
   327→area of architectural concern at PreOne without overlap. Tenant isolation (P1)
   328→is first because a multi-tenant SaaS that leaks data between tenants is not just a
   329→bug — it is an existential business risk; everything else is secondary if isolation
   330→fails. Data integrity (P2) precedes performance (covered implicitly in P5 and
   331→P6) because a fast platform that corrupts data is worse than a slow platform
   332→that preserves it; performance can be optimised, but trust lost to corruption
   333→cannot be regained. Security (P3) and observability (P4) are placed before
   334→simplicity (P5) because PreOne handles child data — a single breach is a
   335→regulatory and reputational event that could end the company. Simplicity is
   336→valued but never at the expense of security or observability. Build for evolution
   337→(P6) reflects the reality that PreOne's product direction will shift; architectures
   338→that lock in current assumptions (e.g., hard-coding academic year as a single
   339→global value) create expensive rework when the assumption breaks. Contracts
   340→before code (P7) ensures that API and data contracts are designed before
   341→implementation, preventing the common failure mode where the contract is
   342→reverse-engineered from the code (and thus inherits all the code's accidents).
   343→Fail loud (P8) rejects silent failure modes — exceptions that are swallowed,
   344→defaults that mask misconfiguration — because silent failures in a child-data
   345→platform are how breaches happen. Automate everything repeatable (P9) is the
   346→principle that justifies the CI/CD, IaC, and testing investments in Volumes 6 and
   347→8. Document every decision (P10) is the meta-principle that justifies the ADR
   348→corpus itself. The most likely counter-argument is that 10 principles are too
   349→many to enforce rigorously. The counter-counter-argument is that enforcement
   350→is not via human vigilance but via the ADR workflow: every Proposed ADR must
   351→list which principles it serves (or explicitly state it does not serve any, which is
   352→itself a flag for ARB review). This makes enforcement mechanical, not
   353→discretionary.

   399→DATABASE IMPACT
   400→No direct database impact. The principles are abstract and do not prescribe
   401→schema, storage, or indexing choices. However, P1 (Tenant Isolation) and P2
   402→PreOne ADR Series - Phase 2 / 10 - Vol 1: Architecture Foundation - 10
   403→
   404→PreOne ADR - Volume 1: Architecture Foundation v3.0
   405→(Data Integrity) directly constrain every database ADR in Volume 3: ADR-043
   406→(Multi-Tenant), ADR-044 (School Isolation), ADR-047 (Soft Delete), and
   407→ADR-048 (Audit Trail) are all implementations of these principles at the data
   408→layer. The principle citation in each Volume 3 ADR's Context section creates a
   409→traceable link from concrete schema choices back to foundational values.
   410→API IMPACT
   411→No direct API impact. However, P7 (Contracts Before Code) is the foundational
   412→principle for Volume 5 (API & Integration). Every API ADR — ADR-091 (REST),
   413→ADR-092 (Versioning), ADR-093 (Error Codes), ADR-106 (Idempotency) — cites
   414→P7 in its rationale. The principle enforces the workflow: OpenAPI specs are
   415→reviewed and merged before implementation PRs are accepted; a PR that
   416→introduces an endpoint not in the spec is blocked by CI.
   417→UI IMPACT
   418→No direct UI impact. However, P4 (Observability) and P8 (Fail Loud) influence
   419→UI behaviour: errors are surfaced to users with clear, actionable messages (not
   420→silent failures); loading states are explicit (not blank screens); offline states are
   421→communicated (ADR-142). P5 (Simplicity) governs UI design system choices in
   422→Volume 7 — component count is kept minimal, composition is preferred over
   423→configuration.
   424→SECURITY IMPACT
   425→P3 (Security by Default) is the foundational security principle. Every Volume 4
   426→ADR must cite P3. The principle means: authentication is required by default
   427→(opt-out requires ARB approval); encryption is enabled by default; audit logging
   428→is on by default; rate limiting is on by default. Opting out of any default security
   429→control requires an ADR that justifies the exception and is approved by both the
   430→Domain Architect and the Security Officer. This inverts the traditional model
   431→where security is added later — at PreOne, security is the starting point and
   432→removal requires justification.
   433→PERFORMANCE IMPACT
   434→P2 (Data Integrity over Performance) explicitly accepts performance trade-offs
   435→in favour of correctness. For example, we choose serialisable transaction
   436→isolation (not read-committed) for financial data, accepting the latency cost. P5
   437→(Simplicity) reduces performance overhead by avoiding over-engineering — no
   438→premature optimisation, no speculative caching. Performance optimisation is
   439→permitted only when (a) a measured bottleneck is documented, (b) the
   440→optimisation does not violate P1-P4, and (c) the optimisation is captured in an
   441→ADR.
   442→SCALABILITY ANALYSIS
   443→PreOne ADR Series - Phase 2 / 10 - Vol 1: Architecture Foundation - 11
   444→
   445→PreOne ADR  -  Volume 1: Architecture Foundation  v3.0
   446→The principles scale with the organisation. At 40 engineers, the 10 principles
   447→are reinforced through code review and team leads. At 150 engineers (3-year
   448→target), the principles are enforced through CI pipeline checks (linting for
   449→security defaults, contract tests for API specs, coverage thresholds for
   450→observability). At 500+ engineers, the principles are embedded in onboarding
   451→and in the architecture review workflow. The ceiling is not the principles
   452→themselves but the enforcement mechanism — which is why ADR-158 (ARB)
   453→and ADR-159 (Decision Governance) exist as the scalability backstop.
   454→OPERATIONAL CONSIDERATIONS
   455→P4 (Observability) and P9 (Automate) have direct operational implications.
   456→Every service must emit structured logs (JSON), metrics (Prometheus format),
   457→and traces (OpenTelemetry) — this is verified at deploy time. Every repeatable
   458→operational task (deployment, backup, restore, scaling) must be automated —
   459→manual runbooks are permitted only for genuinely novel incidents. The on-call
   460→engineer should never perform a task that could have been automated; if they
   461→do, a follow-up ticket to automate it is mandatory.

   647→CONTEXT
   648→When PreOne launched in 2024, the engineering team was 8 engineers building
   649→a single monolith in Spring Boot. By mid-2025, the team had grown to 40
   650→engineers and the monolith was showing strain: deploy times exceeded 15
   651→minutes, merge conflicts were frequent, and the codebase had 200k LOC with
   652→no clear module boundaries. The team considered migrating to microservices.
   653→A 6-week evaluation studied the trade-offs. The team found that PreOne's traffic
   654→(peak ~5,000 requests/second) did not justify the operational overhead of
   655→microservices. The team's DevOps capacity (2 engineers) could not support
   656→20+ services. The business domain — preschool operations — is highly coupled
   657→(enrollment touches billing, attendance, reporting, notifications), and a
   658→distributed system would require complex saga orchestration for transactions
   659→that are currently single-process. The evaluation concluded that the pain
   660→points (deploy time, merge conflicts) were caused by the absence of module
   661→boundaries, not by the monolith topology. The decision was to keep a single
   662→deployable but enforce strict module boundaries — a modular monolith.
   663→BUSINESS DRIVERS
   664→The primary driver is operational efficiency: a modular monolith requires 1
   665→deployment pipeline, 1 monitoring dashboard, 1 on-call rotation, and 1 CI/CD
   666→infrastructure. At PreOne's scale (40 engineers, 5,000 RPS), microservices
   667→would multiply operational cost by 5-10x without proportional business benefit.
   668→The team can ship features faster because cross-module refactoring is a single-
   669→PR operation, not a multi-service coordinated migration. The secondary driver
   670→is transactional integrity: PreOne's core workflows (enrollment, fee collection,
   671→attendance) span multiple domain modules and require ACID transactions. In a
   672→distributed system, these would require sagas with compensating transactions
   673→— a 10x implementation cost and a class of failure modes (partial completion,
   674→compensation failure) that does not exist in a monolith. The tertiary driver is
   675→debugging: a single stack trace from log to database is faster to diagnose than
   676→tracing across 5 services.
   677→PROBLEM STATEMENT
   678→The PreOne codebase has grown to 200k LOC without enforced module
   679→boundaries, causing deploy-time strain and merge conflicts. We must decide
   680→between migrating to microservices (distributed, operationally expensive) or
   681→enforcing module boundaries within the existing monolith (centralised,
   682→operationally lean).

   756→DECISION
   757→ADOPTED
   758→We adopt a modular monolith architecture. The PreOne platform is a single
   759→Spring Boot application deployed as a single container image, with internal
   760→module boundaries enforced through Java package-level access (module-
   761→info.java), ArchUnit dependency rules (ADR-007), and a strict module API
   762→contract. Each module exposes a public API package and hides its internal
   763→implementation. Cross-module access is permitted only through the public
   764→API. The monolith is deployed with multiple replicas behind a load balancer
   765→for high availability (ADR-125) and horizontal scaling (ADR-126).
   766→DETAILED RATIONALE
   767→The modular monolith was chosen because PreOne's pain points were
   768→boundary-related,  not  topology-related.  A 200k LOC  codebase  with no
   769→boundaries is painful regardless of whether it is one process or twenty.
   770→Conversely, a 200k LOC codebase with strict boundaries is maintainable in a
   771→single process. The evaluation showed that the team's pain would reduce 80%
   772→from boundary enforcement alone, with zero operational overhead.   The
   773→decision preserves ACID transactions for core workflows. Enrollment (create
   774→enrollment record, charge fee, send confirmation, update attendance roster) is
   775→currently a single transaction across 4 modules. In a microservices topology,
   776→this would require a saga with 4 compensating actions and a new class of failure
   777→PreOne ADR Series  -  Phase 2 / 10  -  Vol 1: Architecture Foundation  -  19
   778→
   779→PreOne ADR - Volume 1: Architecture Foundation v3.0
   780→(what if the confirmation email is sent but the fee charge is rolled back?). The
   781→business cost of partial-enrollment failures — a parent who paid but has no
   782→enrollment, or an enrollment with no fee — is high enough that the
   783→architectural complexity of sagas is not justified at current scale. The scaling
   784→ceiling is the most-cited counter-argument. The analysis shows PreOne's peak
   785→traffic (5,000 RPS) is well within a single Spring Boot instance's capacity
   786→(typically 10,000-20,000 RPS with optimised JVM settings). At 25,000 RPS
   787→projected peak (5,000 schools), 3-5 replicas suffice — horizontal scaling is a
   788→solved problem. The ceiling where microservices become necessary is around
   789→100,000 RPS, which PreOne will not reach in the 5-year horizon. The team-
   790→discipline concern is real but manageable. ArchUnit tests run in CI and fail the
   791→build on boundary violations. The module-info.java declaration is checked at
   792→compile time. Engineers cannot accidentally cross boundaries — they must
   793→explicitly open a module's API. The discipline is enforced mechanically, not
   794→through vigilance.

   854→DATABASE IMPACT
   855→The modular monolith uses a single PostgreSQL database (ADR-041) shared
   856→across all modules. Each module owns its tables (prefixed with module name,
   857→e.g., enr_enrollments, bill_charges, att_attendance). Cross-module foreign keys
   858→are forbidden — modules reference each other by UUID only, never by FK. This
   859→preserves module boundaries at the data layer: a module can be extracted to a
   860→separate database in the future without FK migration. The schema is managed
   861→by Flyway (ADR-057) with per-module migration files. Read replicas (ADR-041)
   862→serve reporting queries to avoid contending with write traffic.
   863→API IMPACT
   864→The modular monolith exposes a single REST API surface (ADR-091) with per-
   865→module path prefixes (/api/v1/enrollments, /api/v1/billing, etc.). Internally,
   866→modules communicate via in-process method calls through public API
   867→interfaces, not via HTTP. This means internal calls have zero network overhead
   868→and full transactional participation. The public REST API is the only external
   869→surface; internal module APIs are not network-accessible. Versioning
   870→(ADR-092) applies only to the public API; internal APIs can change freely as
   871→long as both sides are updated in the same deploy.

   875→UI IMPACT
   876→The UI is a single React SPA (ADR-131) that calls the monolith's REST API.
   877→From the UI's perspective, the modular monolith is indistinguishable from a
   878→microservices backend — it sees one API host with multiple path prefixes. The
   879→UI does not need to know about module boundaries. Loading states, error
   880→handling, and offline support (ADR-142) are implemented uniformly across all
   881→API calls.
   882→SECURITY IMPACT
   883→The modular monolith simplifies security because there is a single trust
   884→boundary (the API gateway) and no internal service-to-service authentication.
   885→Internal module calls are fully trusted (same process, same JVM). This
   886→eliminates an entire class of security concerns (service-to-service auth, mTLS,
   887→internal token propagation) that microservices must address. However, it
   888→concentrates risk: a single RCE vulnerability compromises all modules. This is
   889→mitigated by defence-in-depth (input validation at every module boundary,
   890→output encoding at every API response, sandboxed file processing).
   891→PERFORMANCE IMPACT
   892→The modular monolith is significantly faster than a microservices equivalent for
   893→cross-module workflows. A typical enrollment flow (4 module calls) completes
   894→in ~5ms in-process, versus ~50ms if each call were a network hop. The
   895→performance ceiling is the single JVM's throughput, which is typically 10,000-
   896→20,000 RPS for I/O-bound workloads. At PreOne's projected peak of 25,000
   897→RPS, 3-5 replicas suffice. JVM warmup is a known cost (~30 seconds for peak
   898→performance); this is mitigated by gradual traffic shifting in the deployment
   899→pipeline (ADR-018).
   900→SCALABILITY ANALYSIS
   901→Horizontal scaling is the primary scaling mechanism: add more replicas behind
   902→the load balancer (ADR-126). Each replica is stateless (session state in Redis,
   903→ADR-073), so scaling is linear up to the database connection pool limit (~100
   904→replicas at current PostgreSQL max_connections=1000 with 10 connections
   905→per replica). Beyond 100 replicas, connection pooling (PgBouncer) extends the
   906→ceiling to ~500 replicas. Vertical scaling (larger JVM heap, more CPU) is the
   907→secondary mechanism. The true ceiling is the database — PostgreSQL single-
   908→instance throughput is ~50,000 writes/second, which PreOne will not approach
   909→in the 5-year horizon.
   910→OPERATIONAL CONSIDERATIONS
   911→Operations are simpler than microservices: 1 deployment pipeline, 1 container
   912→image, 1 health check endpoint, 1 monitoring dashboard. Deployments use
   913→PreOne ADR Series - Phase 2 / 10 - Vol 1: Architecture Foundation - 22
   914→
   915→PreOne ADR  -  Volume 1: Architecture Foundation  v3.0
   916→blue-green strategy (ADR-018) with 30-second traffic shifting. Rollback is a
   917→single image swap. On-call engineers debug a single process — stack traces are
   918→complete, no distributed tracing required for internal calls. The main
   919→operational risk is the 'noisy neighbour' problem: a CPU-intensive operation in
   920→one module (e.g., report generation) can starve other modules. This is
   921→mitigated by separate thread pools per module and by offloading heavy work to
   922→background jobs (ADR-112).

   967→REJECTED ALTERNATIVES
   968→Microservices were rejected because the operational overhead (5-10x) is not
   969→justified at PreOne's scale (5,000 RPS, 40 engineers). The team evaluated the
   970→'team size per service' heuristic (Amazon's 2-pizza rule) and found that with 40
   971→engineers, microservices would yield 4-5 services — too few to justify the
   972→overhead and too many to be a true monolith. The hybrid option (monolith core
   973→+ microservices for notifications/reporting) was deferred to Phase 6/7
   974→evaluation, not rejected outright; if notifications or reporting modules grow to
   975→require independent scaling, they can be extracted later because module
   976→boundaries are already enforced. The service-based option was rejected as a
   977→marginal benefit over modular monolith — the boundary ambiguity (which
   978→service owns this?) and the distributed transaction cost outweighed the
   979→deployment independence.
   980→MIGRATION PLAN
   981→No migration required — PreOne is already a monolith. The v1.0 work (Q4
   982→2025) was to enforce module boundaries on the existing codebase: introducing
   983→module-info.java declarations, refactoring cross-module calls to go through
   984→public API packages, and adding ArchUnit tests. This work is complete. The
   985→v3.0 refresh adds the in-process event bus (ADR-027) and the transactional
   986→outbox (ADR-104) to enable future extraction if needed. If a module is extracted
   987→to a separate service in the future, the extraction is a mechanical refactor:
   988→replace in-process method calls with HTTP/gRPC calls, replace local
   989→transactions with sagas. The module boundaries are already correct; extraction
   990→changes the transport, not the structure.
   991→TESTING STRATEGY
   992→Module boundaries are tested via ArchUnit (unit-level: dependency rules),
   993→integration tests (cross-module API contracts), and end-to-end tests (user
   994→workflows). The test pyramid (ADR-146) applies: ~70% unit tests within
   995→modules, ~20% integration tests across module APIs, ~10% E2E tests.
   996→Contract tests (ADR-149) verify that module public APIs match their
   997→consumers' expectations. Performance tests (ADR-150) verify that the monolith
   998→meets latency targets under load. Boundary integrity is tested daily via CI
   999→ArchUnit runs.

  1004→The monolith emits structured logs (JSON, ADR-119), Prometheus metrics
  1005→(ADR-121), and OpenTelemetry traces (ADR-122). Per-module dashboards
  1006→show request rate, latency, error rate, and resource usage (CPU, memory,

  1009→shows all modules side-by-side for quick comparison. The noisy-neighbour risk
  1010→is monitored via per-module CPU/mem metrics — if one module consistently
  1011→dominates, it triggers an evaluation for extraction.
  1012→FUTURE EVOLUTION
  1013→The modular monolith is the right choice for PreOne's current and 5-year
  1014→horizon. The trigger for re-evaluation is sustained traffic exceeding 50,000 RPS
  1015→or a single module consuming >50% of resources. The likely evolution is partial
  1016→extraction: notifications (already a separate module) may extract to a separate
  1017→service first (it is async, no transactional coupling). Reporting may extract to a
  1018→separate read-optimized service (ADR-060). Core transactional modules
  1019→(enrollment, billing, attendance) will remain in the monolith indefinitely
  1020→because ACID transactions are too valuable to give up. This evolution path is
  1021→captured in ADR-002 v2.0 when triggered.
